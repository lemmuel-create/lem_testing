Concept

LTE operates in licensed spectrum, so the rules come from the service rules for each band (Parts 22, 24 and 27), not Part 15. There's no minimum bandwidth or PSD limit. A device is certified against a radiated power limit, either ERP (relative to a dipole, used below 1 GHz) or EIRP (relative to an isotropic antenna), plus an emission mask. Emissions outside the authorized block must be attenuated by 43 + 10·log10(P) dB, which works out to −13 dBm regardless of transmit power.

The reference bandwidth depends on the band. PCS and AWS (above 1 GHz) use 1 MHz, and in the first 1 MHz outside the block the measurement bandwidth may shrink to 1% of the emission bandwidth. Cellular, 600 MHz and 700 MHz (below 1 GHz) use 100 kHz. Bands 12, 13 and 71 allow at least 30 kHz in the first 100 kHz, and Band 5 allows 1% of the emission bandwidth in the first 1 MHz. Band 13 also has a stricter −35 dBm limit, measured in 6.25 kHz, over the public-safety bands 763–775 and 793–805 MHz. PCS and AWS cap the peak-to-average power ratio at 13 dB.

Method notes
  • Measurements follow KDB 971168: conducted power over the channel, 99% OBW, and 26 dB emission bandwidth (EBW) to set the near-edge measurement bandwidth.
  • Emission mask: for each frequency outside the block, power is integrated over the band's measurement bandwidth: 1 MHz (PCS/AWS, §24.238(b), §27.53(h)(3)) or 100 kHz (Band 5, §22.917(b); Bands 12/13/71, §27.53(c)(5), (g)). Near the edge it uses 1% of the EBW in the first 1 MHz (PCS/AWS, Band 5) or 30 kHz in the first 100 kHz (Bands 12/13/71). Then it compares with −13 dBm, or −35 dBm in 6.25 kHz in Band 13's public-safety ranges. The plot draws the mask in the trace's own units (dBm per 30 kHz).
  • This tool treats the channel edges as the authorized block edges, which is how labs test the lowest and highest channels at the band edges. In service, the block edge is the carrier's licensed block.
  • ERP = EIRP − 2.15 dB. Limits shown are for portable/mobile devices; some bands allow more for fixed or vehicle-mounted stations.
  • Spectral shapes are idealized; the shoulder slider stands in for PA linearity (ACLR).
