Concept

5 GHz Wi-Fi falls under §15.407, not §15.247. The limits depend on the sub-band and, in U-NII-1, on the device class (access point or client). Instead of a minimum bandwidth there's a 26 dB emission bandwidth (B). In U-NII-2A and 2C, the power limit is the lesser of 250 mW or 11 dBm + 10·log10(B), so a wider signal is allowed more power. PSD is limited per MHz.

Outside the sub-band, emissions are limited in absolute EIRP per MHz: −27 dBm/MHz, a stepped mask for U-NII-3, and about −41 dBm/MHz where the neighbor is a restricted band. U-NII-2A and 2C share spectrum with radar, so devices there also need DFS and, at higher power, TPC.

Method notes
  • 26 dB EBW: outermost points 26 dB below the peak, with RBW about 1% of the EBW (KDB 789033). B in MHz feeds the U-NII-2A/2C power formula.
  • PSD: highest trace point scaled to 1 MHz (500 kHz for U-NII-3) assuming a flat, noise-like spectrum. Labs use 1 MHz RBW with an average detector.
  • Out-of-band: trace converted to EIRP per MHz (+10·log10(1 MHz ÷ RBW) + antenna gain). Limit: −27 dBm/MHz, the U-NII-3 mask by distance from the band edge, or −41.2 dBm/MHz in restricted bands (54 dBµV/m at 3 m).
  • Antenna gain above 6 dBi reduces power and PSD limits dB for dB. The fixed point-to-point exceptions (U-NII-1 up to 23 dBi, U-NII-3) and the outdoor-AP elevation limit are noted but not modeled.
  • U-NII-1 and U-NII-2A share one out-of-band boundary (5150–5350 MHz), so emissions from channel 48 into U-NII-2A are not out-of-band. Channels that straddle two sub-bands (for example 160 MHz channel 50) are omitted for clarity.
