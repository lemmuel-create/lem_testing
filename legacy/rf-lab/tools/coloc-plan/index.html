Concept

Putting certified modules into one product doesn't automatically keep them certified. Each module's grant has conditions: which antennas are allowed, its RF exposure assumptions, and usually "not co-located with other transmitters" unless evaluated. Under KDB 996369, the host maker is responsible for showing the final product still complies.

This builder takes the radios, their grants and the way the product is used, and works out three things. First, whether each radio can ride on its module grant, needs a Class II permissive change, or needs its own certification. Second, which simultaneous-transmission radiated tests the intermod planner says are needed. Third, what RF exposure work applies. The result is a test matrix you can hand to the lab.

Method notes
  • Certification path, for each certified module: higher antenna gain than the grant, a different antenna type, portable use when the grant assumes 20 cm or more, or simultaneous-transmission exposure that isn't excluded all point to a Class II permissive change (filed by the grantee) or a change in FCC ID (§2.933). Otherwise the grant covers the radio and the host does an integration check. Own designs need full certification.
  • Simultaneous-transmission rows come from the intermod planner's math (shared/coloc.js): every pair of radios that transmit at the same time and produce a harmonic or intermod product inside a §15.205 restricted band, up to the 10th harmonic.
  • RF exposure uses the same sum-of-ratios calculation as the RF exposure tool: MPE for mobile use, SAR exemption for portable use.
  • Radios count as co-located when antennas are closer than 20 cm and they transmit at the same time. This rule of thumb comes from KDB 447498 and typical grant conditions; always read each module's grant notes.
  • This is a planning aid. The lab and the certification body make the final call, and module grant conditions override anything here.
