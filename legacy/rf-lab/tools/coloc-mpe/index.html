Concept

When radios transmit at the same time, their RF exposure adds up. The FCC rules don't judge each radio alone; they use a sum of ratios. Each transmitter's exposure is divided by its own limit, and the total must stay at or below 1.

How the ratio is computed depends on how close the product sits to people. Mobile or fixed products (20 cm or more) use power density against the MPE limit. Portable products (closer than 20 cm, handheld or body-worn) are exempt from SAR testing only if each radio's available power, as a fraction of the §1.1307 exemption threshold, still sums to 1 or less. Otherwise SAR must be measured, and measured SAR values are summed against 1.6 W/kg.

Method notes
  • Mobile: S = EIRP / (4πr²) (far-field, no ground reflection), time-averaged by duty cycle, compared with §1.1310 Table 1 general-population limits at the lowest frequency of each radio.
  • Portable: available power is the greater of time-averaged conducted power and ERP (§1.1307(b)(3)(i)(B)). P_th = ERP₂₀cm·(d/20)^x for d ≤ 20 cm and ERP₂₀cm for 20–40 cm, with ERP₂₀cm = 2040·f (0.3–1.5 GHz) or 3060 mW (1.5–6 GHz) and x = −log10(60 / (ERP₂₀cm·√f)).
  • When a measured SAR is entered for a radio, it replaces that radio's exemption ratio with SAR ÷ 1.6 W/kg. If the measured SAR sum exceeds 1.6 W/kg, KDB 447498 allows the SAR-to-peak-location separation ratio (SPLSR) or a simultaneous SAR measurement; that step is not modeled here.
  • Radios that never transmit at the same time (for example, time-multiplexed by firmware) can be left out of the sum, but the lab will ask for evidence of the scheduling.
