Concept

2.4 GHz Wi-Fi is certified under the same §15.247 digital-modulation rules as HaLow: at least 500 kHz of 6 dB bandwidth, up to 1 W conducted, and 8 dBm in any 3 kHz. Every 802.11 mode passes the bandwidth test easily. The hard part is the band edges.

Below 2400 MHz, emissions only need to be 20 dB under the in-band peak, until 2390 MHz. Above 2483.5 MHz (and below 2390 MHz) is a restricted band, where emissions must meet an absolute field-strength limit of about −41 dBm EIRP per MHz, however strong your signal is. That's why channels 1 and 11 usually ship with reduced power, and channels 12–13 are rarely used in the US.

Method notes
  • 6 dB bandwidth and PSD follow KDB 558074 (RBW 100 kHz for bandwidth; PSD scaled to 3 kHz from the highest trace point).
  • Restricted-band check: trace level is converted to dBm per 1 MHz (+10·log10(1 MHz ÷ RBW), valid for noise-like emissions), plus antenna gain, giving EIRP. It's compared with 54 dBµV/m at 3 m ≈ −41.2 dBm EIRP (average). The peak limit is 20 dB higher; this tool treats the trace as an average measurement.
  • Labs usually measure band edges radiated with the real antenna, or conducted plus antenna gain as done here (KDB 558074 allows both).
  • Spectral shapes are idealized. The shoulder slider stands in for PA linearity; real margins depend on your PA, filter and power setting.
