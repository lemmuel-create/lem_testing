Concept

CBRS (3550–3700 MHz) is governed by Part 96, a shared-spectrum service. Devices are certified by class. End user devices (EUDs) are limited to 23 dBm EIRP per 10 MHz. Category A base stations (CBSDs) get 30 dBm per 10 MHz and Category B gets 47 dBm per 10 MHz. Because the limit is a density, a 20 MHz channel may radiate 3 dB more total power than a 10 MHz one.

CBSDs are also capped in density: Category A at 20 dBm/MHz and Category B at 37 dBm/MHz. Emissions outside the assigned channel follow a stepped conducted mask. The limit is −13 dBm/MHz within 10 MHz of the channel edge, or within one channel bandwidth for an EUD, and −25 dBm/MHz beyond that. Separately, emissions are capped at −25 dBm/MHz below 3540 and above 3710 MHz, and at −40 dBm/MHz below 3530 and above 3720 MHz. EUDs also need at least 30 dB adjacent channel leakage ratio. CBSDs may transmit only on channels a Spectrum Access System (SAS) grants them, and EUDs only under a CBSD's control.

Method notes
  • EIRP density: power integrated over a sliding 10 MHz window, plus antenna gain minus loss; the highest window is compared with the class limit. For channels narrower than 10 MHz this equals total EIRP.
  • Mask: for each frequency outside the channel, power is integrated over 1% of the 26 dB EBW (first 1 MHz from the edge) or 1 MHz (beyond) per §96.41(e)(3). It is then compared with −13 dBm/MHz (within 10 MHz for CBSDs, within B MHz for EUDs) or −25 dBm/MHz. The §96.41(e)(2) limits also apply: −25 below 3540 / above 3710 and −40 below 3530 / above 3720 MHz.
  • EUD ACLR: channel power minus the higher of the two adjacent same-width channels. PSD (CBSDs): highest EIRP in any 1 MHz.
  • Channel edges are treated as the SAS-assigned channel edges. Certification testing uses the lowest and highest channels in the band.
  • Spectral shapes are idealized; the shoulder slider stands in for PA linearity (ACLR).
