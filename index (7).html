Concept

Under §15.247(a)(2), a digitally modulated system in 902–928 MHz must have a 6 dB bandwidth of at least 500 kHz: the distance between the two points where the spectrum falls 6 dB below its peak. Passing lets the radio transmit continuously at up to 1 W without frequency hopping. The rule makes sure the bulk of the energy is spread out; the separate 8 dBm/3 kHz PSD limit caps how much power lands in any narrow slice.

Measure with RBW 100 kHz, VBW ≥ 3× RBW, peak detector, max hold (KDB 558074). Watch how changing the RBW changes the answer, which is why the procedure fixes it.

Method notes
  • n dB bandwidth: outermost points where the trace falls to (peak − n dB), linearly interpolated.
  • 99% OBW: frequencies enclosing 99% of the integrated trace power.
  • Peak PSD: highest trace point scaled to 3 kHz, P3k = PRBW − 10·log10(RBW/3 kHz). Valid with an average (RMS) detector and a signal wider than the RBW.
  • Band edge: highest level outside 902–928 MHz relative to the in-band peak; restricted-band emissions need separate field-strength limits.
  • Simulator shapes are idealized; use them for training, not as a substitute for lab data.
